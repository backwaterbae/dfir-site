Initial Access Broker (IAB) Model

The Initial Access Broker operates as the first point of entry, establishing access to target infrastructure and then coordinating specialized cyber service providers across multiple jurisdictions. Rather than maintaining in-house technical capabilities for all operations, the IAB leverages a distributed network of specialized vendors, each providing distinct surveillance and cyber capabilities. This architecture enables operational flexibility, attribution obfuscation, and jurisdictional complexity.

Service Categories Identified: The investigation has documented seven distinct service categories procured through the IAB model: (1) Phishing operations via European infrastructure, (2) Digital identity spoofing coordinating with human impersonation networks, (3) Cellular telecommunications interception, (4) Electronic surveillance of devices, (5) GPS-based geolocation tracking, (6) Server compromise and Linux exploitation, and (7) Call capture via SS7 protocols. Evidence suggests 4-year operational timeline (2022-2026) with sustained multi-vendor coordination.

Attribution Complexity: The multi-vendor model creates significant attribution challenges for investigators and targets. Each service provider operates independently with distinct infrastructure, making comprehensive detection difficult. Geographic distribution across France, Southeast US, and unknown jurisdictions further complicates legal jurisdiction and law enforcement coordination.
